How to Use & Exact Formula

3-Step Quick Guide

  1. Enter current bank cash, current monthly revenue, expected monthly revenue growth %, and total monthly expenses.
  2. Compare your Base Runway against the built-in Downside Shock (−30% revenue, 50% slower growth, +20% cost overrun).
  3. Use the Shock Runway to decide when to cut burn, raise prices, or start fundraising (typically 6+ months before zero cash).

Formula & Worked Example

Monthly_Net_Burn(m) = Monthly_Expenses − Monthly_Revenue(m)
Runway = First month m where Cash(m) < 0

With $45,000 cash, $3,500 monthly revenue growing 10%/mo, and $8,500 monthly burn, base runway is ~14 months; under a −30% revenue and +20% expense shock, runway compresses sharply to ~6 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why stress-test runway with a −30% revenue and +20% cost shock?

Early startup revenue is lumpy and infrastructure, ad spend, or contractor costs routinely overrun. Planning solely on base-case runway leaves zero buffer when a channel slows down.

What is the difference between gross burn and net burn?

Gross burn is total monthly cash outflow (expenses). Net burn is expenses minus monthly revenue collected. Runway is governed by net burn.

How many months of runway should a bootstrapped founder keep?

Keep at least 6–12 months of shock-tested runway before committing to fixed recurring expenses like full-time hires or annual office leases.

Does revenue growth automatically extend runway?

Only if unit contribution margin is positive and customer acquisition spend does not scale faster than collected cash.
